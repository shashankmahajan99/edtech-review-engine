3. Five-pass QA assembly line: engineer for zero unsupported changes

First, change the internal quality target from “0% hallucination” to zero known unsupported edits released, with a measured residual-error rate and mandatory escalation for uncertainty.

No responsible AI operator can prove that every generative output will be error-free across all documents. NIST specifically recognizes hallucination, privacy, information integrity, and human-AI interaction as risks requiring active management.

+1

Apex can, however, build a system in which unsupported suggestions are identified, rejected, or escalated before delivery.

Pass 1 — Intake integrity and scope control
Gate 1

Objective: Ensure the system reviews the right document, under the right permissions, within the right scope.

Required checks:

Assign a unique job ID, client ID, version number, and authorized reviewer.

Validate file type, document integrity, readable text, and expected word count.

Preserve an immutable copy of the submitted original.

Confirm the requested service: proofreading, structural feedback, rubric assessment, or a combination.

Flag missing context, incomplete drafts, unsupported attachments, and contradictory instructions.

Remove unnecessary personal identifiers from the review context where practical.

Confirm the client is authorized to submit the material and that applicable privacy and processing terms are in place.

Fail condition: Corrupt, ambiguous, unauthorized, or materially incomplete submissions go to a human queue. They do not enter automated editing.

Pass 2 — AI annotation with evidence constraints
Gate 2

Objective: Generate suggestions without inventing facts, experiences, achievements, or personal history.

For every proposed edit, the engine should generate a structured internal record containing:

Original text span and exact character offsets.

Proposed replacement or comment.

Edit category: grammar, clarity, structure, repetition, or rubric feedback.

Evidence from the submitted draft supporting the suggestion.

Whether the edit changes meaning or introduces a factual assertion.

Confidence and escalation status.

The most important rule: AI may improve expression, but it must not manufacture evidence.

For example:

Original: “I helped organize a weekend food drive.”

Acceptable: “I helped organize a weekend food drive.” A reviewer may flag the sentence for a more specific description if the student can supply one.

Unacceptable: “I led a citywide food drive that served 500 families.” Those details do not exist in the source.

Implement deterministic checks where possible. Reject any generated replacement containing new names, dates, quantities, awards, institutions, or accomplishments unless those details are directly supported by the authorized source material.

Fail condition: A suggestion without a traceable source span or a defensible rationale is discarded or escalated.

Pass 3 — Reviewer verification and voice calibration
Gate 3

Objective: Make the human reviewer a genuine decision-maker, not a rubber stamp.

Reviewer checklist:

Does each edit correct a real problem or offer a clearly justified improvement?

Does the revision preserve the student's intended meaning?

Does it retain distinctive vocabulary, sentence rhythm, tone, and personal perspective?

Does it introduce an experience, motivation, accomplishment, or claim not supplied by the student?

Does the feedback support the student's own revision rather than replacing the student's authorship?

Are rubric scores tied to observable features of the draft rather than speculative judgments about the student?

Require the reviewer to accept, modify, or reject each substantive AI suggestion. High-risk factual and meaning-changing edits should require explicit review.

Build a voice-preservation protocol around the original draft, including a semantic-diff report and an edit-intensity measure. Do not treat similarity scores or AI detectors as proof that a voice has been preserved.

Fail condition: Any invented detail, material change in meaning, or unjustified rewriting triggers rejection and rework.

Pass 4 — Independent quality audit
Gate 4

Objective: Catch errors that the primary reviewer missed.

Use a second reviewer or independent verification mechanism for high-risk changes and a statistically meaningful, risk-weighted sample of ordinary reviews.

Audit for:

Factual additions and contradictions.

Grammar errors introduced by the edits.

Meaning changes and voice drift.

Tracked changes that do not match the approved edits.

Rubric inconsistencies and unsupported scoring rationales.

Missing comments, corrupted formatting, or incomplete output files.

The second pass must not simply ask another model whether the first model is correct. It should compare proposed changes against the original text and explicit acceptance criteria.

For release quality, maintain a benchmark suite of known defects and adversarial cases. Run it whenever the model, prompt, document parser, or tracked-change generator changes.

Fail condition: Any critical defect blocks release and triggers review of related jobs processed under the same version.

Pass 5 — Release validation and post-delivery accountability
Gate 5

Objective: Ensure the client receives the correct, complete, auditable deliverable.

Final checks:

Open and validate the generated .docx file programmatically.

Verify that tracked changes correspond to approved edits.

Confirm the original text is preserved wherever no change was authorized.

Check that the rubric report and Word document refer to the same draft version.

Ensure the correct client branding and document destination.

Confirm that confidential information is not exposed across client accounts.

Record reviewer identity, engine version, timestamps, audit results, and release status.

Provide a clear correction route and service-recovery process.

A document should be released only if all mandatory checks pass. If a check fails, the job returns to the appropriate earlier stage rather than bypassing the gate.

4.4 Capacity planning: do not let sales outrun quality

At a seven-minute reviewer cycle, one reviewer can theoretically process approximately 8.6 papers per hour. That is a mathematical ceiling, not a sustainable full-shift throughput assumption.

Suppose Apex has 40 customers averaging 100 reviews per month. That is 4,000 reviews monthly.

At seven minutes per review, primary reviewer labor alone equals about 467 hours monthly. If you assume 70% productive utilization across 160 paid hours per reviewer per month, you need approximately five full-time-equivalent reviewers for that primary workload.

You must add capacity for second-pass QA, exceptions, leave, training, rework, demand spikes, and operational coverage. Your actual staffing model should use observed end-to-end labor data rather than the seven-minute headline.

A simple operational rule: never accept a new capacity commitment unless forecasted peak demand, reviewer coverage, and quality audit capacity are all funded.

6. Enterprise readiness: the gates before institutional contracts

Before pitching a university or a sophisticated admissions platform, Apex needs a documented answer to five questions.

Where does student data go? Inventory every server, backup, reviewer device, AI provider, and third-party service that touches a document.

Who can access it? Implement tenant separation, least-privilege permissions, secure authentication, access logs, and reviewer confidentiality agreements.

How long is it retained? Define retention periods for originals, working files, final deliverables, and audit records. Test deletion and backup expiry rather than merely writing a policy.

What happens after an incident? Establish a response owner, containment procedure, customer notification process, and documented incident register.

What can you prove? Maintain a current system inventory, access-control evidence, benchmark results, version history, vendor list, and written service-level commitments.

These are operational requirements, not claims that Apex is already certified or compliant with every relevant law. Because you operate from India and may serve US clients, obtain legal review of applicable privacy, contractual, cross-border processing, and consumer-protection obligations.
